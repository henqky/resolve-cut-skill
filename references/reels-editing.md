# Professional Reels Editing

Practitioner guidance gathered from web research (June–Nov 2026 sources). Mostly vendor blogs, so
treat numbers as defaults to test, not platform rules.

## Structure

1. **Hook (first 1–3 s).** Most sources say the opening decides distribution; polish matters less.
   Hook types: a surprising statement, a visual interruption, a relatable pain point, or an
   identity opener ("how I…" beats "how to…"). Start on the payoff, not the setup.
   Sources: [captions.ai – how to make Instagram Reels](https://captions.ai/blog/how-to-make-instagram-reels),
   [Async – 17 tips for retention](https://async.com/blog/how-to-edit-instagram-reels/).
2. **Body.** Cut out dead air. Match cut rate to content energy: fast cuts for hooks and listicles,
   slower for emotional beats. A reframe or zoom every few seconds resets attention; static talking-head
   shots are where viewers drop off. A deliberate half-second pause before a key line can make it feel more real.
   Source: [Social Media Examiner – unpolished reels](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/why-unpolished-reels-win-an-instagram-growth-strategy-for-business/).
3. **Payoff / close.** End on a clear result or a loop-friendly last frame.

## Cutting technique

- **Cut on motion** (hand gesture, head turn) hides the edit better than cutting on a still frame.
- **Jump cuts** are a deliberate style: break a script into 1–2 sentence chunks to create cut points.
- **J-cut:** next clip's audio starts before its picture (builds anticipation).
  **L-cut:** previous clip's audio continues over the next picture (keeps dialogue flowing).
  Both are "split edits". Risk: disorienting viewers, so keep them short.
  Source: [Epidemic Sound – J-cuts and L-cuts](https://epidemicsound.com/blog/j-cuts-and-l-cuts),
  [Adobe – J cut and L cut](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/discover/j-cut-and-l-cut).
- **Beat-synced cuts:** land each cut on a musical beat; zoom the timeline to confirm alignment.
- **Edit order:** lock cuts first, then add text and captions last.

## Captions

- Most viewers watch without sound at first, so captions are essential.
- Two to three words per line, in the middle third of the frame.
- Avoid overused caption templates; match the style to the content.
- Source: [captions.ai](https://captions.ai/blog/how-to-make-instagram-reels).

## Safe zones (9:16, 1080×1920)

Figures below are Meta ad-guidance numbers that third-party guides reuse. Instagram does not publish
organic Reels safe zones in the help pages checked, so use these as a conservative default and preview
on a phone.

| Area | Default clearance |
|---|---|
| Top | ~270 px (keep titles below this) |
| Bottom | ~670 px (captions and CTA above this; the caption bar sits here) |
| Sides | ~65 px |
| Right rail | ~120 px (like, comment, share buttons) |

Sources: [Kapwing – Reels safe zones](https://www.kapwing.com/video-editor/instagram-reels/safe-zone),
[TryMyPost – safe zones 2026](https://www.trymypost.com/blog/instagram-reels-safe-zones-text-placement-2026),
[Hopper HQ – Reel size 2026](https://www.hopperhq.com/blog/instagram-reel-size/).
Guides disagree (bottom clearance 250–670 px); the conservative values above are safest.

## Testing

Run two versions of the same reel with different openings (a trial Reel works as an A/B test)
and see which holds cold viewers.
Source: [Buffer – Reels guide 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-reels-instructions-and-ideas-for-small-businesses/).
